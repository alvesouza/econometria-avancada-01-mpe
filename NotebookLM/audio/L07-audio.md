Explain and discuss in depth Class 7, More econometric models: general error structures, hierarchical models and limited dependent variables. Use the uploaded slides `Aula_class-07_GLM3.pdf`, `Aula_class-07_HLM.pdf` and `Aula_class-07_limiteddependentvariablemodels.pdf`. Pair them with `Livro_Edward Greenberg - Introduction to Bayesian Econometrics (2007, Cambridge University Press) - libgen.li.pdf` (§4.5–4.6, §8.1–8.2, §9.1–9.3, pp. 50–149) and `Livro_Wooldridge J.M-Introductory econometrics. A modern approach-Cengage Learning (2016).pdf` (Ch. 8, §12.1–12.3, §14.1–14.3, §17.1–17.2). The thread: each model relaxes one assumption of the Gaussian linear model of Class 1, and each fits with the Gibbs and Metropolis–Hastings tools of Classes 3+4, usually through data augmentation. It covers:

General covariance: From GLM3, write y = Xβ + ε with ε ~ N(0, σ²Ω). If P'P = Ω⁻¹, then Py = PXβ + Pε is an ordinary regression, which is GLS. Derive the full conditionals: β is normal with V1⁻¹ = V0⁻¹ + σ⁻²X'Ω⁻¹X, and σ² is inverse gamma with the Ω-weighted residual sum of squares. Case I is heteroskedasticity, ωᵢ = exp(γ'zᵢ), which needs a Metropolis–Hastings step for γ. Use the US airlines cost data (6 firms, 15 years, from Greene): the auxiliary regression log e² = −6.3723 + 1.7032·load factor motivates a variance that rises with load factor. Connect this to WLS and robust standard errors in Wooldridge Ch. 8.

Student-t errors: Show that if λᵢ ~ IG(ν/2, ν/2) and εᵢ | λᵢ ~ N(0, σ²λᵢ), then εᵢ ~ t_ν(0, σ²): the t is a scale mixture of normals. Derive the conditional λᵢ | rest ~ IG((ν+1)/2, (ν + εᵢ²/σ²)/2) and explain why outliers get large λᵢ and so less weight. Discuss the exponential prior on ν with mean 25. Geweke's result is that under a flat prior on ν the posterior mean of β need not exist. Link this to Greenberg §8.1.2.

Autoregressive errors: With εt = ρεt−1 + ut and |ρ| < 1, derive the covariance σ²ρ^|i−j|/(1−ρ²), the truncated normal prior for ρ on (−1, 1) and its full conditional. Explain the inverse-CDF draw from a truncated normal, y = μ + σΦ⁻¹(uΦ(b′) + (1−u)Φ(a′)), where a′ and b′ are the standardised bounds. In the simulation, ρ = 0.9 and the GLS start is 0.94. Compare Cochrane–Orcutt and Prais–Winsten in Wooldridge §12.3. Mention SUR (Greenberg §9.1) as the multi-equation version with an inverse Wishart prior.

Hierarchical models: From HLM, contrast three models for panels: pooled (one β), individual effects with a flat prior (fixed effects), and random coefficients αᵢ ~ N(μ, V). Derive the full conditional mean of αᵢ as a precision-weighted average of the unit's own data and μ: partial pooling, the shrinkage of Classes 3+4. Then derive the second-level conditionals: μ is normal around the average of the αᵢ, and V is inverse gamma, or Wishart for the precision when the coefficients are vectors. Say how a small V pushes the model toward pooling and a large V toward separate regressions. Use the airline intercepts, which move from the separate regressions toward the pooled value. Add the eight-schools example (school A: 28.39 with s.e. 14.9) and the nonlinear dugong growth curve μ = α − βγ^x. Mention the weight gain of 68 pregnant women from Gamerman and Lopes (2006). Relate random effects to fixed effects, and say when the Hausman logic of Wooldridge Ch. 14 applies.

Limited dependent variables: Write the Tobit likelihood: densities for the positive observations times F(0 | x)^k for the k zeros. Explain Chib's data augmentation. Draw the latent y* from a normal truncated to (−∞, 0] for each zero, then β and σ² come from the ordinary regression conditionals. In the deck's example, Pr(y = 0) = Φ(−2a/2.062), which is 0.5, 0.25 and 0.1 at a = 0, 0.7 and 1.3. For the probit and ordered probit, use Albert and Chib's latent normal with cut-points drawn uniformly between neighbouring latent values. For the multinomial probit, use utility differences and explain why scale normalisation is needed (McCulloch and Rossi). Use Greenberg §8.2: the Tobit for hours worked in the Mroz data, and probit versus logit for computer ownership. Compare partial effects with Wooldridge §17.1–17.2, and say why the Tobit coefficient is not the effect on observed hours.

Python walkthrough: In words, describe statsmodels WLS, GLSAR and mixedlm, a Tobit likelihood maximised with scipy.optimize, and the same Tobit and probit written as PyMC models, saying what each check should show.

Close with misconceptions: that GLS changes the target parameter, that random effects require normal data, and that OLS on the uncensored observations is fine.
