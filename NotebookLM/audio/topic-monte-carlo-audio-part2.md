Explain and discuss in depth (part 2/2) Markov chain Monte Carlo in Classes 3+4, tied back to the coal-mining change point of Class 1. Use the uploaded slides `Aula_class-03-04_aprendizagembayesiana-bayesianingredients.pdf` and `Aula_class-01_poisson-with-breakpoint.pdf` (the data and its profile likelihood), with `Livro_Edward Greenberg - Introduction to Bayesian Econometrics (2007, Cambridge University Press) - libgen.li.pdf` (Ch. 6–7, pp. 76–107; §8.2.3), `Livro_Ruey S. Tsay - Analysis of Financial Time Series, Third Edition (Wiley Series in Probability and Statistics)-John Wiley & Sons (2010).pdf` (§12.1–12.4, pp. 614–624; §12.7) and `Livro_Morettin, Singer - Estatística e Ciência de Dados (versão preliminar, abril 2021).pdf` (§C.5, pp. 487–488). Part 1 used independent draws. Here the draws are dependent, and the cost of that dependence is the subject. It covers:

Markov chains: For Greenberg's two-state matrix with rows (0.750, 0.250) and (0.125, 0.875), solve πP = π to get (1/3, 2/3). By P¹⁰ the rows are already near (0.33, 0.67), because the second eigenvalue, 0.625, sets the speed. Explain why reducible and periodic chains fail, why an irreducible, aperiodic finite chain converges geometrically to a unique invariant law (Theorem 6.2), and state the ergodic theorem (Theorem 6.3). Show that the AR(1) of Class 1 has invariant law N(0, σ²/(1 − φ²)). MCMC runs this logic backwards: it builds a kernel for a given target.

Gibbs and the switch point: Prove in one line of integration that the Gibbs kernel leaves the joint density invariant. Then build Greenberg's switch-point sampler (7.3)–(7.4) on all 112 years, with Exp(1/2) priors on both rates: each sweep makes two Gamma draws and one draw from a discrete law on 112 years. Integrating the rates out gives P(k | y) in closed form, so the sampler can be graded. The exact P(k = 1891) is 0.240, the years 1886 to 1896 hold 98.1%, and E(λ1 | y) = 3.106 and E(λ2 | y) = 0.930; 20,000 sweeps give 3.107 and 0.931. Explain why the posterior of k is jagged: one year's count, a 4 in 1886 or a 3 in 1896, makes a candidate split better or worse. Estimate the rate densities by Rao–Blackwellisation, averaging the Gamma conditionals over the draws of k (Greenberg p. 105). Compare the Class 1 profile weight of 0.251 over 1885–1895, and discuss when profiling and integrating diverge. Present data augmentation (Tsay §12.1; Morettin and Singer (C.16)–(C.17)): the switch point, Student-t precisions, probit utilities and log-volatilities are all latent blocks.

Mixing and its price: Show that Gibbs on a bivariate normal with correlation ρ makes x1 an AR(1) with coefficient ρ², so the lag-h autocorrelation is ρ^(2h). Derive the inefficiency factor (1 + ρ²)/(1 − ρ²): 1.67 at 0.5, 9.5 at 0.9, 999.5 at 0.999. At ρ = 0.999, Greenberg's 5000 draws give a mean of 1.084 or −0.623 depending on the start. Explain batch-means standard errors (§7.3), why the naive one understates the error by √IF, and the remedies: blocking, reparametrisation and dispersed starts.

Metropolis–Hastings: Derive α(x, y) = min{f(y)q(y, x)/[f(x)q(x, y)], 1} from detailed balance, and show why the normalising constant cancels. For a N(0, 1) target with N(0, s²) random-walk steps, derive the acceptance rate (2/π)arctan(2/s) from a wedge argument: 0.968 at s = 0.1, 0.705 at 1, 0.445 at 2.38 and 0.063 at 20. Argue that neither extreme mixes. For Greenberg's Beta(3, 4) independence sampler with uniform proposals, the stationary acceptance is 0.5726, against his reported 0.57. Prove that Gibbs is MH with acceptance one, which licenses MH-within-Gibbs and Tsay's griddy Gibbs. For the Bayesian logit (§8.2.3), use a random walk scaled by 2.38²/K times the inverse Hessian. On 2000 simulated cases the posterior means (−1.074, 0.850, 0.605) sit beside the MLE (−1.072, 0.845, 0.602), with acceptance 0.32.

Beyond: Name Tsay's stochastic volatility sampler (§12.7) as MCMC with thousands of latent states, a preview of Class 8. Then describe Hamiltonian Monte Carlo: minus the log posterior as potential energy, a random momentum, a gradient-driven trajectory and a final MH correction, the engine of Stan's NUTS.

Python walkthrough: In words, describe a NumPy Gibbs sampler for the switch point checked against the exact P(k | y), autocorrelations and batch means computed by hand, and a random-walk Metropolis loop whose acceptance is compared with the arctan formula.

Close with misconceptions: a long chain is not an informative chain, a rejected proposal still counts as a repeated draw, and agreement among chains from one start proves nothing.
