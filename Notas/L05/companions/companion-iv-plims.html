The Instrument Dial

Schooling x is endogenous: its error εx is correlated with the wage error εy. An instrument z moves x by δ₁ and reaches y only through x, unless φ ≠ 0. Move the endogeneity, the first stage and the exclusion violation, and watch which estimator stays on the true effect β₁. Defaults reproduce Card's OLS 0.0747 and IV 0.1315.

Structure

Causal effect of one more year of schooling on log wage. Every plim line moves with it.

Correlation between the schooling error and the wage error (unobserved ability). At ρ = 0 OLS is consistent.

The instrument

Extra years of schooling for men who grew up near a college. At δ₁ = 0 there is no instrument.

Direct effect of the instrument on log wage, bypassing schooling. IV is off by φ/δ₁.

Every term

Belongs to: Classes 5+6 notes, Notas/L05/ (instrumental variables, L05-05-instrumental-variables.md).

Model: x = δ₁z + εx, y = β₁x + φz + εy, with Var(z) = σz², Var(εx) = σx², Var(εy) = σy², Corr(εx, εy) = ρ and z uncorrelated with both errors (the two-equation system of slide p. 18, intercepts dropped; φ is the exclusion violation of W eq. 15.19, p. 469). Exact probability limits: OLS = β₁ + (ρσxσy + φδ₁σz²)/(δ₁²σz² + σx²); IV = Cov(z, y)/Cov(z, x) = β₁ + φ/δ₁ (slide p. 18, W eq. 15.9, MHE eq. 4.1.3); coefficient on x in the OLS of y on (x, z) = β₁ + ρσy/σx = β₁ + σxy/σx² (slide p. 19; Greenberg §11.2, p. 173). Asymptotic SEs under homoskedasticity: OLS √(Var(y − bx)/(n Var x)) at its plim b; IV √(Var(y − bIVx)/(n Var(x) R²xz)) (W eq. 15.12, p. 466). First-stage F ≈ n R²xz/(1 − R²xz) with R²xz = δ₁²σz²/Var(x).

Calibration: σz = 0.402558, σx = 1.935373, σy = 0.387296 and the defaults β₁ = 0.1315, δ₁ = 0.3199, ρ = −0.2851, n = 3010 are sample moments of card.csv (log wage, education, nearc4) after partialling out the 14 controls of the site's example1-iv-card.html, computed in Notas/L05/L05_python_checks.py. At the defaults the page gives the site's OLS 0.0747 and IV 0.1315 (W Table 15.1, p. 474: .075 and .132). The negative ρ is what the Card numbers imply: OLS below IV.
