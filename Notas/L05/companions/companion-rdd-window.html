The Discontinuity Window

Monthly death rates of Americans aged 19 to 23, the 48 cells of the site's MLDA example. A sharp design switches legal drinking on at 21. Narrow the window around the cutoff, bend the trend, let the slopes differ on each side, or move the cutoff somewhere nothing happens, and watch the estimated jump and its standard error respond.

What is regressed

Car crashes should react to legal drinking; deaths from disease (internal causes) should not.

The estimator

Only cells with |age − a₀| ≤ b enter the fit. At 2.00 every cell is used.

Order of the age trend: linear (6.1.2) or quadratic.

On lets the trend differ left and right of the cutoff, MHE (6.1.6).

The design

Where the treatment dummy switches on. Anything but 21.00 is a placebo test: no rule changes there.

Estimated jump ρ̂ at the cutoff (deaths per 100,000), ± 1.96 SE

The data, the window and the two fitted pieces

cells in the window and the fit   cells outside   jump at a₀   age 21, the real cutoff.

The jump against the bandwidth

Same outcome, order, slopes and cutoff; ρ̂ ± 1.96 SE for every bandwidth on a 0.05 grid. A narrow window trusts the trend less and the data more: lower bias, more variance (slide p. 36).

Every term

Belongs to: Classes 5+6 notes, Notas/L05/ (regression discontinuity, L05-07-regression-discontinuity.md).

Model: OLS on the cells with |a − a₀| ≤ b of ya = α + ρDa + Σk≤p γk(a − a₀)k [+ Σk≤p δk(a − a₀)kDa] + εa, with Da = 1(a > a₀) as in the site's code (over21[age>21]=1). Centring at a₀ makes ρ the jump at the cutoff in every specification (MHE §6.1, p. 192). With p = 1, no interactions and all cells it is the slide's ya = α + ρDa + γa + εa (p. 29); with p = 2 and interactions it is the quadratic of slide p. 31. SE: classical OLS, σ̂² = SSR/(n − k); the band uses 1.96. "Jump in σ units" is ρ̂/σ̂, the site's legend ("MVA: 3.411 stdevs"). Restricting the sample to a window with a uniform kernel is the local linear regression of slide p. 36 (MHE p. 193 weights by distance; a uniform kernel is the simplest case). Normal equations solved by Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting.

Data: Aula/class-05-06/materials/age-mva-internal.txt (48 cells, ages 19.07–22.93, deaths per 100,000; Angrist and Pischke, Mastering 'Metrics, Fig. 4.5, as used on slides pp. 33–35), embedded below as literal values. Site numbers reproduced: linear MVA jump 4.534 (SE 0.768, 3.411 σ), quadratic 4.663 (3.508 σ); internal causes 0.392 (0.380 σ) and 1.073 (1.026 σ).
