# Classes 5+6 — Introduction to causal inference · Map of the notes

> **One claim.** **A causal effect is a contrast of potential outcomes, and data identify it only through an
> assumption the data cannot check.** **The graph decides what to condition on:** forks must be blocked,
> chains out of the treatment and colliders must not be — which is why the same Simpson table is read
> stratified for gender and pooled for post-treatment blood pressure. **Regression inherits every failure of that
> choice:** leaving out a confounder moves the coefficient by γ·δ, a noisy regressor shrinks it by
> $\sigma_x^2/(\sigma_x^2+\sigma_u^2)$, and a bad control reintroduces selection bias. **When no set of observables will do,
> design supplies the variation:** an **instrument** that moves the treatment and nothing else (Card: 0.1315 vs OLS
> 0.0747), a **control group** with the same trend (Card–Krueger: DiD +2.75 FTE), or a **cutoff** with a smooth
> counterfactual (MLDA: +4.53 deaths per 100,000 at 21). Each design trades one untestable assumption for another.

## The one result to hold

$$\boxed{\;\underbrace{E[Y\mid A=1]-E[Y\mid A=0]}_{\text{what the data compare}}=\underbrace{E[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid A=1]}_{\text{ATT}}+\underbrace{E[Y(0)\mid A=1]-E[Y(0)\mid A=0]}_{\text{selection bias}}\;}$$

> **Every method in the unit is a way to make the last term vanish.** Randomisation sets it to zero by design;
> adjustment for confounders (note 02) zeroes it within cells of $L$; OVB (note 03) is its regression form; IV
> compares groups defined by the instrument, whose untreated outcomes are equal by exclusion; DiD assumes the
> difference in $Y(0)$ is constant over time and differences it out; RD assumes it vanishes at the cutoff.

  ([[../../Aula/COURSE|Aula/COURSE]]): expect the bias formulas (OVB, attenuation, OLS under endogeneity), the IV ratio and the
  failure of adding $z$, the DiD and RD identification arguments, d-separation on a small graph, and readings of the site's
  Card, Card–Krueger and MLDA printouts.

- **Named only:** matching and doubly robust estimators; causal discovery; fuzzy RD; data-driven bandwidths; Bayesian
  nonparametric RD (NBA draft); the slides' Bayesian DiD/RDD; Bayesian IV (Greenberg §11.2, the Lopes papers on slide p. 20).
- **Belongs to other classes:** posterior simulation and priors (Classes 3+4); panel fixed and random effects
  (W Ch. 14, MHE §5.1: syllabus topic 03, not on the site schedule) and hierarchical models (Class 7).
