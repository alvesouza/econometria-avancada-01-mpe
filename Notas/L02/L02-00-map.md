# Class 2 — Introduction to statistical learning · Map of the notes

> **One claim.** **Under squared loss the target is the regression function $E[Y\mid X]$**, and
> every learned estimate of it misses a new $Y$ by **squared bias + variance + irreducible noise**.
> **Flexibility trades the first two**, so the test error is U-shaped while the training error only
> falls — which is why **the training error can never choose the model** and cross-validation
> (hold-out, LOOCV, $k$-fold) estimates the test error instead.
> **Classification is the same problem with 0–1 loss:** logistic regression models
> $P(y=1\mid x)$ directly, discriminant analysis builds it from $p(x\mid y)$ by Bayes' theorem, and
> **the cut-off is a statement about costs, not a constant 1/2.**
> **Counts get a log link:** Poisson regression reads coefficients as rate ratios and ties the
> variance to the mean — **a tie that must be checked.**

Three layers per topic:

| Layer | What it means here |
|---|---|
| **U** — intuition | prediction vs inference; why a straight line fails on a 0/1 response; why counts are not Gaussian; the shape of the bias–variance curves |
| **C** — course (examinable) | the decomposition (21) of note 01 with its proof; the coefficient readings of note 02; hold-out, LOOCV with its one-fit shortcut, $k$-fold and the bootstrap band; the logit likelihood, score, Hessian and IRLS; the Bayes and ML discriminant rules, LDA/QDA, misallocation probabilities, unequal costs, ROC; the Poisson GLM, rate ratios and overdispersion — **all with the decks' own numbers** |
| **G** — graduate | the training-error optimism $2\sigma^2(d+1)/n$ (proved, as the root of $C_p$/AIC); separation and non-existence of the logit MLE; smoothing splines, trees, the one-standard-error rule, nested CV, Fisher's canonical directions, the GLM template. **Named or proved, not examinable** |

## The one result to hold

$$\boxed{\;E\big[(Y-\hat\psi(X))^2\big]=\underbrace{\int\operatorname{Viés}^2[\hat\psi(x)]\,dF_X(x)}_{\text{falls with flexibility}}+\underbrace{\int\operatorname{Var}[\hat\psi(x)]\,dF_X(x)}_{\text{rises with flexibility}}+\underbrace{\sigma^2}_{\text{untouchable}}\;}$$

> **Every other note is a way to estimate this, or to trade its terms.** Note 03 estimates the left
> side from held-out data; LDA vs QDA (note 05 §8) is a bias–variance choice; and the classification
> version replaces squared loss by 0–1 loss, whose minimiser is the **Bayes rule**
> $\arg\max_k\pi_kp_k(x)$ — the classification analogue of $E[Y\mid X]$.

## Scope and ceiling

- **Examinable (C):** the results of notes 01–06 as derived there — no code in the final exam
  ([[../../Aula/COURSE|Aula/COURSE]]: *"You will NOT be asked to write R script or similar"*), so
  expect the derivations, the hand arithmetic of the printouts, and the readings.
- **Proved but above the line (G):** the optimism $2\sigma^2(d+1)/n$; separation; the GLM template
  (note 06 §6); the closed-form equal-variance AUC.
- **Named only:** smoothing splines (the Fundamentos learner), trees, boosting and random forests,
  the classification bias–variance and $k$-NN of Fundamentos slides 36–50, the one-standard-error rule,
  nested CV, Fisher's canonical discriminants (LD1/LD2).
- **Belongs to later classes:** ridge, lasso and elastic net (slides 26–27, 32–35 of the logistic
  deck; `cv.glmnet`) and every Bayesian part — the Bayesian logit (slides 23–25), the admission
  script's SIR posterior, the blog page's grid posterior, the Mroz page's Bayes factors — are
  **Classes 3+4**; probit and multinomial models are **Class 7**.
- **Out:** unsupervised learning (clustering, PCA).
