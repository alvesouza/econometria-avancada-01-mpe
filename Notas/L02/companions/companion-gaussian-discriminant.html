The Two-Gaussian Classifier

Two normal populations, a prior and a cost of each mistake. The Bayes rule allocates x to class 1 where π₁c₂₁p₁(x) beats π₂c₁₂p₂(x). Unequal variances bend the boundary into a quadratic with two cut-offs; equal variances straighten it into one.

Class 1 x | y = 1 ~ N(μ₁, σ₁²)

Centre of the class-1 population; moves its density and drags the cut-offs with it.

Spread of class 1. When it differs from σ₂ the log-likelihood ratio is quadratic in x.

Class 2 x | y = 2 ~ N(μ₂, σ₂²)

Centre of the class-2 population. Equal means and equal SDs make the classes indistinguishable.

Spread of class 2. Set it equal to σ₁ to get the linear (LDA) rule.

The decision

Share of class 1 in the population, π₂ = 1 − π₁. At 0.50 with equal costs the Bayes rule is the ML rule.

How much worse it is to send a true class 1 to class 2 than the reverse. Log scale from 0.1 to 10; the middle is exactly 1.

Total misclassification probability  π₁p₂₁ + π₂p₁₂ under the Bayes rule

Model: x | y = k ~ N(μk, σk²), priors π₁, π₂ = 1 − π₁. Discriminant function h(x) = log p(x | y = 1) − log p(x | y = 2) (slide 49). Bayes rule with costs: allocate to class 1 iff h(x) > log(π₂c₁₂ / (π₁c₂₁)), where cij is the cost of allocating to i when the truth is j (slide 51). The deck prints the cost-free threshold log(π₂/π₁) on slide 49; the cost version is the rule that minimises the Bayes risk r(d, π) = Σ πk Σ cjk pjk of slides 51–52. Written out, h(x) − log(π₂c₁₂/(π₁c₂₁)) = ax² + bx + c with a = 1/(2σ₂²) − 1/(2σ₁²), b = μ₁/σ₁² − μ₂/σ₂², c = μ₂²/(2σ₂²) − μ₁²/(2σ₁²) + log(σ₂/σ₁) − log(π₂c₁₂/(π₁c₂₁)) (slide 45 for the ML case). The cut-offs are its exact roots (two, one when σ₁ = σ₂, or none); pij = ∫ φi(x) p(x | y = j) dx (slide 50) is a sum of normal-CDF differences, with Φ evaluated by its Taylor series below |z| = 3 and the Laplace continued fraction above (absolute error below 1e-14). Defaults are the deck's example μ₁ = 1, μ₂ = 5, σ₁ = 6, σ₂ = 2.5 (slide 46); its equal-variance case σ₁ = σ₂ = 6 is slide 47; the Mahalanobis form of the linear rule is slide 48.

Books: ISLP (James et al., 2023) §4.4 Generative Models for Classification, pp. 146–161: LDA for p = 1 (§4.4.1, pp. 147–150), QDA (§4.4.3, pp. 156–158).
