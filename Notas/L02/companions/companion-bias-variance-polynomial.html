The Flexibility Dial

Turn the polynomial degree up and watch squared bias fall while variance rises. Expected test error is their sum plus the noise floor σ², and training error keeps falling past the point where test error turns back up.

The learner

Order of the OLS polynomial in x. It fits d + 1 coefficients, the analogue of the spline's gl. The maximum is min(15, n − 2) so the fit never interpolates.

The training sample

Number of design points, equally spaced over 8 to 18 years of schooling. More points shrink the variance at every degree.

Standard deviation of ε in thousand R$ per year. It scales the variance and sets the irreducible floor σ².

Belongs to: Class 2 notes, Notas/L02/ (bias–variance decomposition, L02-01-prediction-error-bias-variance.md; polynomial learner, L02-03-cross-validation-motorcycle.md).

Model: Y = f(X) + ε,  f(x) = 45 tanh(x/1.9 − 7) + 57,  ε ~ N(0, σ²), x in years of schooling, Y in thousand R$ per year (Fundamentos.pdf slide 21). Decomposition E[(Y − f̂(X))²] = ∫ bias²[f̂(x)] dFX + ∫ Var[f̂(x)] dFX + σ² (slides 16–18); trade-off and Monte Carlo design (slides 19, 25–27); training error falls monotonically (slides 28–30).

What this page changes: Fundamentos fits a smoothing spline (smooth.spline, flexibility gl) to random X ~ U(8, 18) and approximates every term by Monte Carlo. This page uses the polynomial learner of the motorcycle deck (motorcyle-polynomialregression.pdf slides 4 and 11) on a fixed design xi = 8 + 10(i − 1)/(n − 1), so every term is exact, conditional on the design: E f̂(x) = b(x)′(B′B)⁻¹B′f,  Var f̂(x) = σ²b(x)′(B′B)⁻¹b(x), with b(x) an orthonormal polynomial basis built by modified Gram–Schmidt (with re-orthogonalisation) on Legendre polynomials of u = (x − 13)/5 ∈ [−1, 1]. Bias² and variance are averaged over a 400-point uniform grid on [8, 18] (the U(8, 18) average). Expected training error = σ²(1 − (d + 1)/n) + ‖(I − H)f‖²/n, H the hat matrix. No simulation runs in the page.

Books: ISLP (James et al., 2023) §2.2.2 The Bias-Variance Trade-Off, pp. 31–34; §7.1 Polynomial Regression, pp. 290–292.
