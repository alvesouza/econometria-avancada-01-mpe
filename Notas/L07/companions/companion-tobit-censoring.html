The Censoring Dial

A latent y* = βx + u, u ~ N(0, σ²), is seen only through y = max(0, y*). Where x sits (mean a) decides how much of the sample piles up at zero; β and σ decide how the three conditional means bend, and how badly OLS on the censored data understates β.

Latent model

Effect of x on the latent y*, the coefficient a Tobit reports. Slide 9 uses 2.

Noise in y*. It enters every partial effect, so it is not "ancillary" (W p. 539). Slide 9 uses σ² = 0.25.

Covariate

x ~ N(a, 1). Lower a pushes more of the population below the corner. Slide 9 shows a = 0, 0.7, 1.3.

Where the pointwise probabilities, means and partial effects are read.

Share of the population censored at zero  P(y = 0) = Φ(−aβ / √(β² + σ²))

Three conditional means against x

E(y* | x) = βx  E(y | x) = Φ(z)βx + σφ(z) (17.25)  E(y | y > 0, x) = βx + σλ(z) (17.24)  population OLS line of y on x; z = βx/σ.

Marginal distribution of the observed y

y* ~ N(aβ, β² + σ²) over the population. bar: the point mass P(y = 0), drawn to the probability scale on the right  density of y on y > 0  the censored part of the latent density.

Every term at x₀

Belongs to: Class 7 notes, Notas/L07/ (Tobit, L07-05-tobit.md).

Model: y* = βx + u, u | x ~ N(0, σ²), y = max(0, y*) (W eqs. 17.18–17.19, p. 537; slide 5's censoring rule), x ~ N(a, 1) (slide 9), no intercept as on the slide. Population: y* ~ N(aβ, β² + σ²), so P(y = 0) = Φ(−aβ/√(β² + σ²)) — slide 9's 0.5, 0.25, 0.1 at a = 0, 0.7, 1.3 with β = 2, σ² = 0.25 (√4.25 = 2.062). At x₀ with z = βx₀/σ: P(y > 0 | x₀) = Φ(z) (17.21); E(y | y > 0, x₀) = βx₀ + σλ(z), λ = φ/Φ (17.24); E(y | x₀) = Φ(z)βx₀ + σφ(z) (17.25); ∂E(y | y > 0, x)/∂x = β{1 − λ(z)[z + λ(z)]} (17.26); ∂E(y | x)/∂x = βΦ(z) (17.30). Population OLS slope of y on x = Cov(x, y)/Var(x) = β·P(y > 0): (x, y*) is bivariate normal, so Stein's lemma gives Cov(x, max(0, y*)) = Cov(x, y*)·P(y* > 0) (derived in note 05). Its intercept is E(y) − slope·a, with E(y) = Φ(m/s)m + sφ(m/s), m = aβ, s = √(β² + σ²). Φ: Taylor series below |z| = 3, Laplace continued fraction above; λ for z < −3 is the continued fraction itself, so it never divides two underflowing numbers.

Books: Wooldridge 6e §17.2 The Tobit Model for Corner Solution Responses, pp. 536–543; Greenberg (2007) §8.2.1 Tobit Model for Censored Data, pp. 117–122.
