# Class 7 — More econometric models · Map of the notes

> **One claim.** **Every model of this class is the normal linear regression with one twist**, and the
> twist is handled by **adding something to the Gibbs sampler**: a known Ω is a rotation (GLS); a
> variance function needs one **Metropolis** step; Student-t errors become normal once each observation
> carries its own **latent variance**; AR(1) errors are one parameter ρ and **quasi-differencing**.
> **Hierarchical models** put a prior with estimated mean and variance on unit-level coefficients, so each
> unit's estimate is a **partial-pooling** average of its own data and the population.
> **Limited dependent variables** — Tobit, probit, ordered and multinomial probit — are regressions on a
> **latent** $y^*$ seen only through a cut: draw $y^*$ from a **truncated normal** and it is OLS again,
> but **coefficients are latent-scale effects**, not effects on what is observed.

Three layers per topic:

| Layer | What it means here |
|---|---|
| **U** — intuition | why noisy or correlated observations should count less; fat tails as random variances; pooling vs separate fits; corners vs censoring vs truncation; a cut throws away the scale |
| **C** — course (examinable) | GLS (5) and the general-Ω full conditionals of note 01; the scale-mixture derivation and the four conditionals of note 02; $\Omega_\rho$, its tridiagonal inverse, Prais–Winsten, the ρ conditional and the truncated-normal draw of note 03; the three panel models, every hierarchical conditional and the partial-pooling weight of note 04; the Tobit likelihood, data augmentation and (17.24)–(17.30) of note 05; the probit identification, Albert–Chib, the 1.6 rule, the ordered-probit cut-point update and the multinomial probit as a SUR of note 06 — **with the slides' and the books' own numbers** |
| **G** — graduate | closed-form OLS/GLS variance ratios under AR(1) errors and regressors; the Stein-lemma attenuation $\beta P(y>0)$; Geweke's moment conditions; slow mixing of cut-points; MNP identification schemes; multivariate probit. **Proved or named, not examinable** unless the slides state them |

## The one result to hold

$$\boxed{\;\theta\mid\text{rest}\sim N\Big(\big(V_0^{-1}+\sigma^{-2}X'\Omega^{-1}X\big)^{-1}\big(V_0^{-1}\beta_0+\sigma^{-2}X'\Omega^{-1}y^{*}\big),\ \big(V_0^{-1}+\sigma^{-2}X'\Omega^{-1}X\big)^{-1}\Big)\;}$$

> **One conditional, five disguises.** $\Omega$ known or $h(z,\alpha)$: note 01. $\Omega=\mathrm{diag}(\omega_i)$ with latent
> $\omega_i$: note 02. $\Omega=\Omega_\rho$: note 03. $y^*$ = data with the hidden part imputed: notes 05–06. And with
> "β" the unit coefficients and "$\beta_0,V_0$" the population mean and variance, it is the partial-pooling
> update of note 04. What changes between models is only **what else is drawn** around it.
