The Bayes-Factor Dial

Michaela and Lila each toss the same coin m times. Model M1 says the way they toss makes no difference (one probability θ); model M2 gives each girl her own probability. Move the head counts, the sample size and the prior, and read the evidence for M1 as a Bayes factor.

The data

Each girl tosses m times. Table 3.2 uses m = 10 and m = 100.

The priors

Prior on each probability, log scale from 0.1 to 20. α = 1 is Greenberg's uniform prior; large α says every coin is close to fair.

log₁₀ B₁₂ against Lila's heads

Michaela's count and m held at their current values. Dotted: Jeffreys thresholds ±0.5, ±1, ±1.5, ±2 (Table 3.1). Gold: current s₂ and s₂ = s₁.

What each model believes after the tosses

M1: θ | y  M2: θ₁ (Michaela)  M2: θ₂ (Lila). Beta posteriors; the further apart the two M2 curves, the worse M1 fits.

Read-outs

Marginal likelihoods of the observed sequence (no binomial coefficients; they cancel in B₁₂).

Model: M1: θ₁ = θ₂ = θ ~ Beta(α, α); M2: θ₁, θ₂ independent Beta(α, α). Marginal likelihoods m₁ = B(α + s₁ + s₂, α + 2m − s₁ − s₂)/B(α, α) and m₂ = B(α + s₁, α + m − s₁)B(α + s₂, α + m − s₂)/B(α, α)²; B₁₂ = m₁/m₂ (Greenberg (3.4)); P(M1 | y) = 1/(1 + (p₂/p₁)/B₁₂) from (3.3). With α = 1 these are Greenberg's Γ-function expressions on p. 38, including (3.5). The large-sample approximation shown for comparison is log B₁₂ ≈ log LR + ½ log(2m) (p. 37, d₁ − d₂ = −1, n = 2m tosses); it drops the prior and |V| terms, which stay bounded as m grows.
