The Change-Point Dial

British coal-mining disasters, 1851–1962. Move the cut year and the sample, and watch the two Poisson rates, the profile likelihood of the cut and the likelihood-ratio statistic respond.

The cut

Last year of the high-rate regime: years a…τ get rate λ₁, years τ+1…b get λ₂.

The sample

Start of the years used. Every likelihood, rate and test uses years a…b only.

End of the years used. Trimming the tail changes how much evidence the low-rate regime carries.

Weights and intervals

w(τ) is normalised over the cut years 1890−h…1890+h that lie inside a…b−1. The deck uses h = 5.

Level of the Wald intervals λ̂ ± z1−α/2√(λ̂/n) drawn as bands.

The statistic is not referred to a χ²₁ distribution. The cut τ was chosen by searching over years, so the regularity conditions behind Wilks' theorem fail and 3.84 is not a valid critical value (Bolfarine & Sandoval §6.5).

Yearly disasters and the two fitted rates

λ̂₁ on a…τ   λ̂₂ on τ+1…b   single rate on a…b (dashed). Bands are the Wald intervals; faded years are outside the sample.

Profile log-likelihood of the cut year

ℓp(τ) − max over every admissible τ in a…b−1. Shaded: the weight window. Dashed gold: τ = 1890, the deck's 40/72 split.

Fitting the Poisson model, as on slide 14

Dispersion test: (n−1)s²/ȳ ~ χ²n−1 under the Poisson model, upper-tail p-value.

Belongs to: Class 1 notes, Notas/L01/ (L01-03, coal-mining change point).
Model: yt ~ Poisson(λ₁) for t ≤ τ, Poisson(λ₂) for t > τ; λ̂j = sj/nj; ℓp(τ) = s₁ log λ̂₁ − n₁λ̂₁ + s₂ log λ̂₂ − n₂λ̂₂ (with 0·log 0 = 0; the constant −Σ log yt! cancels everywhere); single rate ℓ(λ̂) = s log λ̂ − nλ̂; LR = 2{ℓp(τ) − ℓ(λ̂)}; w(τ) = eℓp(τ)/Στ′∈W eℓp(τ′). Every value is computed exactly from the 112 counts; z and the χ² tail are numerical (inverse-normal with a Halley step; regularised incomplete gamma).
