# Class 1 — Review of basic ingredients · Map of the notes

> **One claim.** **A likelihood picks the estimator; the sampling rule decides how it behaves.**
> The same $\theta^s(1-\theta)^{n-s}$ gives one MLE and one posterior, but different biases and
> p-values depending on whether $n$ or $s$ was fixed. **Estimators are judged by their sampling
> distribution, through MSE = variance + bias²**, worked out exactly, by Monte Carlo, or by the
> bootstrap. The bootstrap fails exactly where the statistic lives on the edge of the support.
> **Models are judged by what they predict and what they assume.** One Poisson rate fails the
> coal data's $E=V$ test and two rates fit. $R^2$ cannot choose a regression. An AR(1) has an
> equilibrium distribution only when $\lvert\phi\rvert<1$. **And a p-value is not the
> evidence.** $p=0.036$ can sit beside $P(H_1\mid x)=0.31$, and a search over 32 specifications
> makes "significance" likely by construction.

Three layers per topic:

| Layer | What it means here |
|---|---|
| **U** — intuition | the two experimenters with one coin; "the bootstrap cannot recover what the sample does not contain"; the eye reading the coal series; the replication-crisis narrative; reading regression output |
| **C** — course (examinable) | the decks' and Bolfarine's definitions and results with their numbering: likelihood, MLE, sufficiency, unbiasedness, consistency, Cramér–Rao efficiency, MSE, bootstrap, dispersion test, profile likelihood, GLRT; OLS in matrix form, OLS = MLE, $R^2$/$\bar R^2$/$C_p$/AIC/BIC; AR(1) moments, equilibrium law, unit root, AR(p), VAR(1) mean and vec covariance; family-wise error, Bonferroni, Bayes factor vs p-value |
| **G** — graduate | Haldane's unbiased estimator, UMVUE and invariance, $V(s^2)$ from cumulants, the integrated change-point likelihood, the best multiple $c\,x_{(n)}$, the Jeffreys–Lindley paradox in full. **Named or proved, not examinable** |

## Reading order

### Part I — likelihood and the properties of an estimator

1. [[L01-01-likelihood-sampling-rules]] — `mle_sampling_rules.pdf` slides 1–11; B §1.3, §2.1,
   §3.1–3.2 (pp. 4–45, selected), §6.3 (p. 100); G §2.2 (pp. 12–18). **Binomial and
   negative-binomial data share one likelihood, MLE, $J(\hat\theta)$ and posterior, but not their
   bias (0.1 vs 0.256) or their p-values (0.0107 vs 0.0020).** Haldane's unbiased fix, and why the
   Wald interval leaves $[0,1]$.
2. [[L01-02-poisson-mle-properties]] — Poisson deck slides 1–12; B §1.1.4, §2.1–2.2, §3.1–3.2, §3.7
   (pp. 2–52, selected). **$\hat\lambda=\bar y$ passes all four tests: sufficient, unbiased,
   consistent, efficient.** $s^2$ is unbiased too, but only $1/(1+2\lambda)=20\%$ efficient. The
   Monte Carlo table, and Chebyshev's vacuous bound.

### Part II — a model that fails, two that fit, and a first prior

3. [[L01-03-coal-mining-change-point]] — Poisson deck slides 13–21; B §3.4 (pp. 46–49), §6.5
   (pp. 103–114); G §7.1.1 (pp. 91–95). **Overdispersion (index 1.592, $p<0.001$) rejects one rate.
   The profile likelihood puts the cut at 1891 (weight 0.251), with LR = 70.0, which is not
   referred to $\chi^2_1$ because $\tau$ was searched.** Profiling vs integrating.
4. [[L01-04-first-bayesian-poisson]] — handwritten "first Bayesian experience"; G §2.2
   (pp. 12–18). **Exp(1/2) prior × Poisson likelihood = Gamma$(s_n+1,a+n)$. On 1851–1891 the
   posterior mean is 3.084 and the mode 3.060, against the MLE 3.098: shrinkage with weight
   $a/(n+a)\approx1\%$.**

### Part III — judging estimators: MSE, Monte Carlo, bootstrap

5. [[L01-05-comparing-estimators-mse-bootstrap]] — MSE deck slides 1–20; handwritten MSE note;
   B §1.1.5, §1.3 (pp. 3–13), Ex. 2.2.5, Ex. 3.1.3; I §5.2 (pp. 212–215). **MSE = Var + bias². For
   $U(0,\theta)$, $\hat\theta_4<\hat\theta_2<\hat\theta_3<\hat\theta_1$ for $n\ge3$, with ties at
   $n=1,2$. The nonparametric bootstrap puts a 63% point mass on $x_{(n)}$, so it is inconsistent
   for the maximum, and the parametric bootstrap fixes it.**

### Part IV — regression and time series

6. [[L01-06-gaussian-linear-regression]] — `multiplelinearregression.pdf` pp. 1–22;
   `overfitting_a_linear_model.html`; `regressao_abstencao_2026.pdf`; W Ch. 2–3, §6.3, §8.2; AME
   §1.5.1.1, Ch. 2, §3.1–3.2. **OLS is a projection, and the Gaussian MLE. $R^2$ cannot fall, so
   it cannot select. $R^2_{in}=1.0000$ predicts 10.5 times worse. The abstention table is read
   line by line (HC1, joint $F$, an $R^2$ gain of 0.002).**
7. [[L01-07-ar-var-review]] — `ar-ma.pdf` pp. 1–17; `ar-var-R.txt`; T §2.1–2.4 (pp. 30–57),
   §8.2 (pp. 399–403); W §11.1, §11.3. **AR(1) = a regression on the lag, and OLS = the conditional
   MLE. For $\lvert\phi\rvert<1$, $y_t$ settles into $N(\mu,\sigma^2/(1-\phi^2))$. At $\phi=1$ the
   variance grows like $t$. VAR(1): eigenvalues, $(I-\Phi)^{-1}\phi_0$, and the vec formula on
   Tsay Ex. 8.3.**

### Part V — evidence

8. [[L01-08-p-hacking-evidence]] — `part1-p-hacking.pdf`; `p_hacking_slides-economics.pdf`.

## The one result to hold

$$\boxed{\;\underbrace{L(\theta\mid x)}_{\text{picks }\hat\theta}\ \longrightarrow\ \underbrace{\text{sampling distribution of }\hat\theta}_{\text{set by the design: exact, MC or bootstrap}}\ \longrightarrow\ \underbrace{\text{MSE}(\hat\theta)=V(\hat\theta)+B(\hat\theta)^2}_{\text{the verdict}}\;}$$

> **The first arrow obeys the likelihood principle. The second does not.** That one fact
> explains the stopping-rule paradox (L01-01), the biased-but-better $x_{(n)}$ (L01-05), the
> bootstrap's failure at the edge of the support (L01-05 §10), the searched LR that is not
> $\chi^2_1$ (L01-03 §6), and the minimum of 32 p-values that is not a p-value (L01-08).

## Scope and ceiling

- **Examinable (C):** what the decks and Bolfarine's assigned sections state. That covers the
  likelihood, MLE and observed/expected information for Bernoulli, binomial, negative binomial,
  Poisson and uniform; sufficiency, unbiasedness, consistency and Cramér–Rao efficiency; MSE and
  the four $U(0,\theta)$ estimators; Monte Carlo; the nonparametric and parametric bootstrap; the
  dispersion test, profile likelihood and GLRT; the Exp–Gamma update; OLS in matrix form, OLS =
  MLE, $R^2$, $\bar R^2$, $C_p$, AIC, BIC and best subset; AR(1) moments, estimation, equilibrium
  law and unit root; AR(p) as a regression; VAR(1) stationarity, mean and covariance;
  family-wise error, Bonferroni, and Bayes factor vs p-value.
- **Named only (G):** Haldane's estimator and its proof, UMVUE and Rao–Blackwell, invariance and
  the delta method beyond the deck, the integrated change-point likelihood (L01-03 §8), the best
  $c\,x_{(n)}$, exact (Clopper–Pearson) intervals, and the Bayesian sample-size criterion.
- **Belongs to later classes, not developed here:** the Gibbs switch-point model (G §7.1.1,
  Classes 3+4); ridge, lasso, elastic net, normal–gamma and horseshoe (the later pages of
  `multiplelinearregression.pdf`, Classes 3+4); `hierarchical-beta-binomial.html` (Classes 3+4);
  Bayes factors for composite hypotheses (Classes 3+4); Dickey–Fuller tests and ARCH/GARCH
  (Class 8); impulse responses and structural VARs (Class 9); cross-validation (Class 2).
- **Final exam:** no code. Expect derivations and the reading of printed output
  ([[L01-09-formulas]] §0).

## Errata found

- **Bolfarine & Sandoval, Table 1.1 (p. 12)** prints $\text{MSE}[x_{(n)}]=\theta^2/662$ at $n=10$
  and $\theta^2/2312$ at $n=20$. Formula (17) gives $\theta^2/66$ and $\theta^2/231$, and the ratio
  column should read 0.36, 0.18, 0.059, 0.017. The book's conclusion is unaffected →
  [[L01-05-comparing-estimators-mse-bootstrap]] §5.
- **Handwritten MSE note** (`Class1-comparing-estimators-MSE.pdf`, p. 2) states the ranking "for
  $n\ge2$" with strict inequalities. It holds strictly for $n\ge3$, since at $n=2$,
  $\text{MSE}(x_{(n)})=\text{MSE}(2\bar x)=\theta^2/6$. The same page writes the MSE definition as
  $E\{(\hat\theta-E(\theta))^2\}$ → [[L01-05-comparing-estimators-mse-bootstrap]] §2, §5.
- **p-hacking deck** (`part1-p-hacking.pdf`, slide 5) counts "$2^5=32$" specifications. Its own
  table of options has $3\times3\times3\times2\times3=162$ combinations, for which the
  independent-test rate is 0.9998. The 0.81 is also an upper bound, because the specifications
  share the same 30 students → [[L01-08-p-hacking-evidence]] §3.
- Minor: the handwritten Poisson table rounds $\Pr(y=3\mid2)=0.18045$ to 0.181
  ([[L01-02-poisson-mle-properties]] §2). `bootstrap-example.html` prints the $\sigma^2$ interval
  upper limit first ([[L01-05-comparing-estimators-mse-bootstrap]] §12.2). The economics deck
  misnames the authors of Brodeur et al. (2016) ([[L01-08-p-hacking-evidence]] §6).
