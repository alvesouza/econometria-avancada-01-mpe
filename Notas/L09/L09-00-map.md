# Class 9 — Multivariate time series · Map of the notes

> **One claim.** **A VAR is a multivariate regression**: OLS equation by equation is its MLE, the
> companion matrix decides stationarity, and impulse responses and forecasts are powers of it.
> **Its shocks are correlated, so every structural reading needs restrictions** — $q(q-1)/2$ of them —
> and the familiar Cholesky ordering is one such set, not a neutral device. **Its parameters grow like
> $q^2p$**, and the two answers of the lecture are shrinkage (the Minnesota prior, whose posterior is
> ridge regression toward a random walk) and factors (a few common components carrying the
> cross-section, in a FAVAR or in the covariance). **When coefficients and volatilities move**, each is
> a state and the Kalman filter updates it; **when only covariances move**, DCC models correlations
> directly while factor SV derives them from the factor's volatility.

## The one result to hold

> **Every structural or forecasting statement from a VAR is a function of $(B,\Sigma)$ plus an
> assumption.** $\Psi_s$ and forecasts need only $(B,\Sigma)$; orthogonalised IRFs, FEVDs and
> "structural" equations need an ordering or other restrictions on $A_0$ ($\Sigma=(A_0'A_0)^{-1}$ leaves
> $q(q-1)/2$ degrees of freedom); and with many series, $B$ itself needs a prior.
